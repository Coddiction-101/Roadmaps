# Network Penetration Tester

## Job Profile Summary

A Network Penetration Tester, often called a **Pen Tester** or **Ethical Hacker**, simulates cyberattacks on a company's computer networks and systems to find vulnerabilities before real attackers do. Their role is to help organizations understand and fix weaknesses that could lead to data breaches or other security incidents.

## What Do Network Penetration Testers Do?

- **Simulate attacks:** Attempt to test defenses on networks, including firewalls, routers, switches, wireless networks, and servers.
- **Identify vulnerabilities:** Use tools and manual techniques to find weaknesses such as unpatched software, open ports, weak passwords, or poor configurations.
- **Test vulnerabilities:** Attempt to exploit discovered weaknesses in a controlled manner, without damaging systems, to demonstrate the risk.
- **Document findings:** Write detailed reports for technical and non-technical stakeholders, explaining risks and how to fix them.
- **Retest:** After fixes are implemented, perform follow-up tests to confirm the vulnerabilities are resolved.

## Penetration Testing Process (Life Cycle)

1. **Reconnaissance:** Gather information about the target network, such as IP ranges, open ports, and network services.
2. **Scanning:** Use tools to identify vulnerabilities in network devices, servers, or systems.
3. **Exploitation:** Test identified vulnerabilities to demonstrate possible unauthorized access or privilege escalation.
4. **Post-exploitation:** Analyze which data or systems were accessible and how multiple vulnerabilities could be chained to gain deeper access.
5. **Reporting:** Create a comprehensive report covering findings, tested vulnerabilities, potential impact, and recommendations.

## Real-Life Example

Imagine a hospital hires a network penetration tester to help protect patient data. The tester discovers that a network printer has an open port and outdated firmware, which could provide a path to sensitive hospital databases. In the report, the tester explains the risk and recommends updating the firmware and closing unnecessary ports, helping the hospital prevent a data breach.

## Key Concepts and Terms

- **Black-box testing:** The tester has no internal information and simulates an outside attacker.
- **White-box testing:** The tester has detailed internal information, such as configuration files or credentials.
- **Gray-box testing:** The tester has partial internal knowledge.
- **Pivoting:** Using one compromised device or system as a starting point to access other devices deeper in the network.
- **Vulnerability chaining:** Exploiting multiple weaknesses in sequence to reach important assets.
- **Social engineering:** Using tactics such as phishing to trick employees and gain access.
- **Privilege escalation:** Gaining higher levels of permission on a system after initial access.

## Prerequisites

### Education and Experience

- A bachelor's degree in Computer Science, IT, or a related field can be useful; some roles may accept proven skills and experience instead.
- Entry-level cybersecurity experience, such as SOC Analyst or IT Support work.

### Technical Skills

- Strong understanding of computer networks, including TCP/IP, VPNs, firewalls, and network protocols.
- Familiarity with offensive security tools such as Nmap, Nessus, Burp Suite, and Metasploit.
- Basic programming or scripting knowledge, such as Python, Bash, or PowerShell.
- Knowledge of operating systems such as Windows and Linux.

### Soft Skills

- Analytical and creative problem-solving.
- Clear communication to explain technical findings to business stakeholders.

### Certifications

- **OSCP:** Offensive Security Certified Professional.
- **CEH:** Certified Ethical Hacker.
- **GPEN and GXPN:** Advanced GIAC penetration-testing certifications.
