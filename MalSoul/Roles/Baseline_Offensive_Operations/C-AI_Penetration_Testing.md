# AI Penetration Testing

## Job Profile Summary

AI Penetration Testing focuses on identifying and testing vulnerabilities in AI-based systems, including machine learning models, chatbots, LLMs, vision systems, and their supporting infrastructure.

As AI technologies are used more widely in businesses and products, testers need to understand how attackers could exploit flaws unique to AI or apply traditional hacking methods to AI-powered systems.

## What Do AI Penetration Testers Do?

- **Test AI systems:** Assess how attackers might trick models, expose data, or manipulate AI behavior through AI code, inputs, API integrations, or backend systems.
- **Find AI-specific weaknesses:** Look for issues such as prompt injection, data poisoning, and model inversion, along with traditional vulnerabilities such as SQL injection in AI APIs.
- **Analyze and adapt:** Combine automated tools with human judgment to test systems, analyze AI behavior, and adapt to detected defenses.
- **Document and report:** Create reports explaining findings, risk levels, and practical fixes for both AI and traditional parts of the system.

## Example of AI Penetration Testing

A tester evaluates a chatbot powered by a large language model (LLM). By crafting prompts, they test whether the chatbot can be made to reveal sensitive internal data. They also check whether maliciously structured input can bypass safety filters and cause unwanted behavior or data disclosure.

The tester reports the weaknesses and recommends stronger prompt filtering and better isolation of sensitive data.

## Key Concepts and Must-Know Terms

- **Prompt injection:** Using crafted inputs to try to bypass an AI model's controls or make it disclose information.
- **Data poisoning:** Adding malicious data to AI training sets to corrupt model outputs or introduce hidden weaknesses.
- **Model inversion:** Attempting to extract sensitive training or proprietary data from an exposed model.
- **Attack surface mapping:** Identifying possible entry points and interactions, such as APIs, user prompts, and data pipelines.
- **Adversarial attacks:** Using specially crafted inputs to cause AI models to make mistakes or expose data.
- **Algorithm exploitation:** Studying how a machine-learning model works to find weaknesses that could be targeted.
- **Automation and AI tools:** Using automated tools to test systems at scale or identify patterns that may be difficult to find manually.

## Prerequisites

### Education and Experience

- Background in cybersecurity, data science, or software engineering.
- Experience working with machine-learning or AI systems and their architecture.
- Penetration-testing or security-research experience is highly valued.

### Technical Skills

- Understanding of machine-learning and AI concepts, such as model training, inference, and datasets.
- Strong programming or scripting skills, commonly in Python.
- Familiarity with penetration-testing tools for AI and traditional environments.
- Familiarity with AI security frameworks such as the OWASP LLM Top 10.

### Certifications and Skill Builders

- AI security certifications are still uncommon; relevant courses and AI security training are increasingly sought after.
- Participation in AI security communities or relevant bug bounty programs.

### Soft Skills

- Creativity and adaptability.
- Analytical and problem-solving ability.
- Clear communication with both technical and non-technical stakeholders.

## Why AI Penetration Testers Are Important

As businesses rely more on AI, new threats can emerge that traditional security teams may miss. AI Penetration Testers focus on risks tied to data-driven and adaptive technology, helping make AI-powered products and services more secure and trustworthy.

> **Authorization note:** Test only AI systems you have explicit permission to assess, and stay within the agreed scope.
